CM4 / CM5 Carrier Board

Professional

Compact 120 × 40 mm carrier for Raspberry Pi CM4 and CM5 with NVMe storage, Gigabit Ethernet, PoE, USB-C PD and peripheral expansion on a six-layer PCB.

Specification
Form factor120 × 40 mm
PCB6-layer, after a 2-layer prototype
ModulesRaspberry Pi CM4 and CM5
PowerPoE via SDAPO DP5405 Powered Device module (25 W, 5 V at about 5 A), USB-C PD (15 V / 3 A profile), local 5 V and 3.3 V rails
InterfacesM.2 2260 M-Key NVMe (PCIe), Gigabit Ethernet, USB, microSD, UART, I²C (Qwiic), 4-pin PWM fan
RevisionsRev 1 (production, GitHub repository) and Rev 2 (GitHub repository). Rev 2 adds the NVMe fix, CSI and HDMI (Hirose connectors) and an I²S daughtercard.
Role
Sole designer, from requirements through schematic capture, six-layer PCB layout and prototype bring-up. Chose the components and placement within the size and cost requirements. Handled BOM, sourcing, supplier communication and PCBA ordering.
Challenge and decision
NVMe worked on the CM5 and failed to link on the CM4. The working CM5 cleared the PCIe routing, which pointed to control signals that differ between modules. In Rev 2 the M.2 enable state is derived from the carrier power state and the wake signal is pulled up.
Result
NVMe operates on both CM4 and CM5 in Rev 2.

PowerUSB-C PD input, 5 V rail designed for about 8 A

Sole designer, from architecture through schematic capture, power design, PCB layout and design verification. Chose the components and placement within the size and cost requirements. Prepared the Rev A release for PCBA manufacture.

The 260-pin breakout, two PCIe links, Ethernet, USB and an 8 A switching supply share one dense region. Placement follows interface topology and the switching node stays clear of the PCIe channels. Rev A was released for 8-layer PCBA manufacture.

03

RF Fingerprinting

Academic

CNN-based RF fingerprinting classifier for a pre-recorded 12-device Zigbee dataset, quantised with HLS4ML and deployed on a Xilinx ZCU111 RFSoC.

Specification
PlatformXilinx ZCU111 RFSoC, inference in programmable logic over AXI Stream and DMA
DatasetPre-recorded Zigbee dataset, 12 devices, 128 × 99 STFT spectrograms
ModelCNN, 28,806 parameters, 653,516 before compression
Precisionap_fixed<24,10> (Q10.14), reuse factor 128, 100 MHz clock
AccuracyAbout 99% for the Python and HLS4ML models, 97.55% for the deployed model on the RFSoC
Latency and utilisation3 to 4 ms per inference, 17.75% LUT, 9.85% DSP, 4.44% BRAM
Role
Master of Engineering thesis project at Macquarie University. Covered CNN training and compression, HLS4ML quantisation, Vitis HLS and Vivado integration, and benchmarking on the ZCU111.
Challenge and decision
The 653,516-parameter CNN was too large for the fabric, so its filters and dense units were cut to reach 28,806 parameters. A 16-bit fixed-point format overflowed and dropped accuracy to 15.2%. The selected configuration is 24-bit Q10.14 with 70% of the compressed model's weights pruned to zero and reuse factor 128, which trades latency for DSP usage.
Result
The deployed model reaches 97.55% with all inference in programmable logic. Throughput is about 250 to 300 samples per second, set by the reuse factor, and STFT preprocessing runs offline.

04

STM32 Data Acquisition

Academic

FreeRTOS firmware on an STM32F429 using DMA and double buffering to receive four 460800-baud UART streams without data loss.

Specification
MCUSTM32F429 Discovery, ARM Cortex-M4
Input4 × UART, up to 460800 baud each, about 180 kB/s aggregate payload
Data pathDMA reception into double buffers, ISRs signal FreeRTOS tasks
VerifiedLossless on 4 channels at 460800 baud
Limit foundAt 921 kbps one channel lossless, 256-byte loss on the other three
Role
Two-person final year project team at NUST (SEECS), 2022. My part was the multi-UART data acquisition interface, with FreeRTOS firmware written in STM32CubeIDE.
Challenge and decision
Handling four 460800-baud streams byte by byte means about 184,000 interrupts per second. UART reception uses DMA into double buffers, so the CPU processes one block while DMA fills the other. ISRs only signal FreeRTOS tasks, and logging stays outside the acquisition path.
Result
Lossless reception on all four channels at 460800 baud, checked with counter data plotted in MATLAB. At 921 kbps one channel stayed lossless and the other three lost 256 bytes when a double-buffer handoff missed its deadline.
